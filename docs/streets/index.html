LA Street Rules

Find a block in the City of Los Angeles to see the parking rules its tickets point to: the street-sweeping day, hours and weeks, how often meter officers come by, and what else gets ticketed there.

Not a guide to parking illegally. This page doesn't suggest parking anywhere, or at any time, the rules don't allow. It shows when and where LADOT has written tickets, so the rules on each block and how they're enforced are easier to understand. Past tickets don't predict the next one: an officer can come by at any time.

Street sweeping signs. Since March 2021, LA sweeps each street every other week (the 1st & 3rd or the 2nd & 4th time its weekday comes up in a month), but many signs still show only the day, like "No parking Thursday". LADOT says parking is legal on a street's off weeks and that it doesn't ticket then; a few tickets still get written, and LADOT says it dismisses those. The weeks on this page are from StreetsLA's list of posted routes, and 98% of sweeping tickets across the city fell on them. For the official schedule, look up your street at streets.lacity.gov.

What the page loads. The map images come from OpenStreetMap and the block data from this site, which GitHub hosts, one small area at a time, so both can tell roughly which part of the city you're looking at. If you use your location, it only moves the map; the exact spot isn't sent anywhere.

      How this was measured, and what it can't tell you

      Data
      LADOT parking citations (data.lacity.org, dataset 4f5p-udkv) from the last two years, rebuilt every week.
      Blocks and sides
      Each ticket's address is matched to the City of Los Angeles street centerlines (LA GeoHub), which list the house numbers on each side of every stretch of street. A block is one street's hundred block, and its line on the map is the part of the street those numbers cover. Tickets written at an intersection, like "5th St / Wall St", have no house number and are left out, and so are streets outside the City of Los Angeles. The centerlines also say which side of the street has the even numbers, so the card names the compass side.
      Sweeping schedule
      The posted day, weeks and time come from StreetsLA's map of posted sweeping routes. Each route covers an area and is swept on two days, one for each side of the street. A side gets the route day that matches the weekday of most of its sweeping tickets, among the routes those tickets sit inside. A side needs at least 8 sweeping tickets for that. A few routes, like Downtown's, are swept every week; one with no fixed time is left out. Tickets written outside the posted time aren't counted. "Ticketed on X% of sweep days" counts posted days with at least one ticket, leaving out holidays and any day with no sweeping tickets anywhere in the city.
      Meter patrols
      Monday to Saturday, 8 am to 8 pm, holidays left out. A visit is a group of meter tickets on one block with no gap over 10 minutes. An officer who finds every meter paid leaves no ticket, so the visit rate is adjusted for those empty visits, assuming the number of expired cars an officer finds on a visit is random (Poisson). A block needs at least 20 meter tickets. Which blocks have meters comes from LADOT's meter inventory (dataset s49e-q6j2).
      Limits
      A ticket only appears when someone broke the rule, so quiet blocks look less enforced than they are, and a rule nobody was ticketed for doesn't show up at all. Addresses that couldn't be matched are left out. Routes and staffing change, and none of this is a promise about next week. Always read the posted signs, and for sweeping, the city's schedule.
